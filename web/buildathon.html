The rules.

Anything not answered here, email hi@joinbuilders.org.

  • Who can enter

    Any current student, at any school in any country. Register with your school email.

  • Teams

    Up to two people, or enter on your own. Each person is on one team.

  • Where you build

    Anywhere. You can be virtual for the whole thing, from kickoff to the final. Kickoff and the closing ceremony are in person at Ohio State, but you don't have to attend either one to compete or to win.

  • When the problems come out

    Every track opens online at 6:00 PM Eastern on Nov 20, the moment kickoff starts. Sign in on this page and open a track for its brief and starter files. No team sees a problem before then.

  • Tracks

    Enter as many tracks as you like. One team can win more than one.

  • Tools

    Use any tools you like, AI included, unless a track's page says otherwise.

  • Scoring

    Every submission is scored automatically, and the live leaderboard shows each team's best practice score. Each track's page gives its metric, its tie rule and how many practice runs you get an hour.

  • The final

    Submissions close at 11 AM Eastern on Nov 22. Your final entry for a track is the submission you pick, or your best-scoring one if you don't pick. See your track's page for how its final is scored. Organizers verify the top results before announcing them.

  • Prizes

    The best final score in each track wins $5,000, and second place gets nothing. Teams anywhere in the world can win. Winners are paid online, in the room or not.

  • License

    Anything built in this hackathon must be released under the MIT License. Builders may show your team's name, demo, screenshots and project description to promote the Buildathon.

  • Code you bring

    You can use open-source libraries and code you wrote before the weekend. If your track takes a repository, name them in its README.

Submit your work

Hand in your entry on the track page. Your team’s list shows your place in the queue as the scorer builds and scores it.

Submission limits
Each track accepts one entry per team at a time, including entries waiting in line. Every submission counts toward the track’s hourly limit, even a rejected one. The form shows how many you have left and when the next one frees up.

See your result

Open your submissions on the track page to read the score and feedback. If an entry is rejected, you’ll see why. Your best score stays.

Choose your final entry
Pick the submission you want in the final before the cutoff. If you leave it unpicked, your best-scoring entry goes through.

Read the leaderboard

Find your team on the leaderboard. Each track has its own board. Practice standings update every few seconds. Only the final decides the prize.

How to read the scores

Expand a board to see every team’s rank and best score. The board names the metric and tells you which direction is better. An earlier submission wins a tie.

The Baseline row is a simple reference entry’s score. Teams above it have beaten it. Once a track publishes its final results, its practice standings fold beneath them.

Ask for help

Sign in to ask a question in leaderboard chat. For a scoring delay, check your track’s queue on the status page.

Chat and announcements
You post under a handle that keeps your name and team private. Announcements appear on the same page. The status page also shows the scoring fleet.

Finish on Sunday

Submissions close Nov 22 at 11 AM ET. Your final entry locks then.

Closing ceremony
Meet in Hitchcock Hall 131 at 12 PM ET for the closing ceremony. Each track’s winner takes $5,000, and the best video entered on your team page takes $100.

  1. Mech Royale by Builders

    Hand in a GitHub repository with a Dockerfile at its top. 4 submissions an hour.

    Final: The top 32 teams on the practice board play a qualifying round, and the top 16 play a 64-match final series on new seeds (last 4 live). The series alone decides.

  2. Shopping Sessions by Databricks

    Hand in a GitHub repository with a Dockerfile at its top. 2 submissions an hour.

    Final: Your chosen commit is rebuilt and timed 3 times on about 60 million new events, and the fastest entries are retimed alone on one idle machine. Aim for under about 60 seconds in practice: if any final run goes over 3 minutes, you get no final score.

  3. CAD De-ID by Machina

    Hand in the S3 folder of one attempt. 2 submissions an hour, 20 scored attempts in all.

    Final: The board at the cutoff is final: your best attempt keeps its score, and nothing new is graded at the cutoff.

  4. Blast Radius by RWX

    Hand in a GitHub repository with a Dockerfile at its top. 1 submission an hour.

    Final: Your chosen commit is scored on unseen changes, and the top entries are rescored one at a time on one idle machine. Only the final counts.
